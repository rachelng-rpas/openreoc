### Parallel event — change of legal entity name (same ACN)

The company changed its name while the tethering application was in the queue.
CASA treated this as a **separate application** (Form 101-02) and reissued the
ReOC; it did not change the tethering assessment.

**No interview was required** — the "CRP interview or Q&A" step was the written
scenario questionnaire plus two written feedback rounds.

### The approval letter

- Approves the **significant change to documented practices and procedures**
  under Part 101 MOS **10A.01**, and names the **Operations Manual version and
  date** that was assessed.
- States CASA was satisfied the change is consistent with the nature and scope of
  the ReOC and would have no adverse effect on aviation safety.
- States the significant change **does not require a re-issue of the ReOC**.
- The inspector confirmed that for a significant-change-only application,
  **acceptance of the manual constitutes the approval** for the proposed
  tethered operations.
- No operating conditions were listed in the letter; the inspector's operational
  reminder was the on-site AMSL check above.

## Lessons Learned

- Beware of the forms used for submission, will cause significant delays if wrong form is used.
- **Significant vs non-significant change.** CASA's guidance (see the
  [CASA page on significant and non-significant changes](https://www.casa.gov.au/rpas-reoc-significant-change-approval-or-notification-non-significant-changes))
  says significant changes need CASA's approval **before** you implement them
  (fees typically apply), while non-significant changes are notified within 21
  days of implementation (no fee). Inserting procedures you have never been
  assessed for is one of CASA's examples of a significant change. A change to
  the ReOC itself or to key personnel goes on Form 101-02.
- **Expect to wait, then expect speed.** No progress updates are sent for
  individual applications; CASA asks applicants not to email for status and to
  use its published service delivery timeframes. The long wait was before an
  inspector was assigned; the assessment itself was quick.
- **Read the inspector's wording exactly.** "Please ensure X is considered and
  complied with" was an acknowledgement, not an instruction to rewrite the
  manual, and "you may include" meant optional. Misreading these would have
  meant unnecessary manual changes.
- **CASA's template procedures are a guide, not a prescribed standard** — but you
  are expected to compare each template section with your manual and insert what
  is missing.
- **Put clause references and a second source in the first submission.** The
  second feedback round was all about evidence: the MOS clause for the height
  limit, a second elevation source, and a re-check of a distance.
- **Check every number against the MOS, not an older manual or a figure.** Our
  manual gave the black area as extending further either side of the runway
  centreline than MOS 4.05(3)(b) (500 m either side). It was conservative, but
  it disagreed with the correct scenario answer.
- **Elevation data needs two sources, and the conservative one wins.** A
  re-check changed one site's elevation between submissions; the change and the
  reason had to be explained and every document updated. CASA also warned that
  mapping tools can differ — confirm height in AMSL on site.
- **Restructuring a manual breaks cross-references.** After moving sections,
  re-render the document and check section numbers, cross-references and the
  Amendment Record.
- **Check the approval letter against your document.** The letter must name the
  manual version and date that was approved. In this case the date listed was a
  day earlier than the manual's cover date, which is worth confirming with CASA
  rather than assuming it is a typo.
- **Name changes run on their own track.** A name change with the same ACN is a
  separate application, needs the form signed by an officeholder, and the manual
  must show the current legal entity name — but CASA was happy to receive one
  revised manual covering both, provided the revision log shows everything.
- **Answer every point in one reply**, with evidence attached. Each exchange
  with a regulator costs days.
